# Code layout

Back to the [README](../README.md).

Where the code lives, and where a new feature's pieces go. The office used to grow by adding to the middle of a few big files: the server's message switch and the page's store. Each of those is a registry now, and a feature is a module of its own plus one line in each list it joins. If a change has you editing the middle of `server.ts`, the store or `protocol.ts`, it probably belongs somewhere else.

There are three parts: `src/client` (the pages, built by Vite), `src/server` (Node) and `src/shared` (types and pure code both use, with no Node imports).

## Client

Every page is an entry of its own in `vite.config.ts`, an HTML file with its script beside it in `src/client`: the 1D view (`lite.html`, `lite.ts`, at `/lite`: where every project opens, on its Command Center), the 2D Office view (`pixel.html`, `pixel.ts`, at `/pixel`, reached only by the 1D view's **Go to Office**), Home (`home.ts`), The Firm (`firm.ts`), the docs (`docs.ts`), the phone version (`m.ts`), first-run setup (`setup.ts`) and the sign-in pages (`login.ts`, `claim.ts`, `join.ts`). The 3D office that used to be at `/` (`main.ts`, three.js, and its `core/`, `features/`, `input/`, `world/`, `player/`, `sound/` and `models/` folders) is gone: `/` redirects to the 1D view (`server/http/routes/pages.ts`). `tests/client-structure.test.ts` checks it stays gone and that every module under `src/client` is loaded by one of the pages.

### Folders

- **`state/`** is the store. `store.ts` declares the core fields (who you are, the people, the floors, and the floor you're on with its workers, screens, boards and queue), and `core.ts` keeps them up to date. Everything else is a slice in `state/slices/`: a module that adds its fields and topics to `Store` and `Topics` (`declare module '../store'`), sets where they start (`init`), and says what it takes in from each server message (`on`) and from each floor you arrive on (`enter`). Slices run in the order of `SLICES` in `state/slices/index.ts`, which is the order their topics fire in, so a new one goes at the end. A page follows a topic with `store.on('<topic>', fn)`. What the browser remembers between visits is `persist.ts`.
- **`ui/`** is the pages' shell: the ☰ menu (`menu.ts`, its items `menuitems.ts`), the views' buttons (`viewpick.ts`: Go to Office and Return to Project) and the windows (terminals, changes, the queue, and the GitHub windows in `ui/github/`). ⚙️ Settings is `ui/settings/`: a builder per group of settings (`you.ts`, `notify.ts`, `workers.ts`, `building.ts`, `project.ts`, `office.ts`, each giving back its nodes and an `off`), and the full page (`page.ts`, the 1D view's ⚙️ Settings tab; `flat.ts` wires it to a flat session and sends the 2D view and the home page there). Its sections, their order and their addresses (`/lite?tab=settings&section=<id>`) are `src/shared/settings-sections.ts`: a new section is a row there and a builder in `SECTION_BUILDERS` (the typecheck wants both), and every settings link uses `settingsHref`. Each module imports its own stylesheet (`import './palette.css'`), as a feature's `ui.ts` does (`import './ui.css'`).
- **`shared/`** (`src/client/shared/`) is what the pages share: the tab title and hiring, and what the 1D view (`lite.ts`, at `/lite`) and the 2D Office view (`pixel.ts`, at `/pixel`) both use: their connection and coming in (`session.ts`), what they do with a worker (`workers.ts`), and the floor picker (`floors.ts`). The home page (`home.ts`, at `/home`) is in `src/client/home/`: the project cards (`projects.ts`), their ⏸ / ▶ state icons and the one Pause all / Resume all button (`run-state.ts`, its pure logic in `run-state-logic.ts`) and the statistics (`stats.ts`, from `GET /api/home/stats` in `server/home-stats.ts`). The 2D Office view is in `src/client/pixel/`: the camera (`camera.ts`), the still office and its props (`office.ts`, `props.ts`), the people and desks drawn every frame (`people.ts`), what can be clicked (`hotspots.ts`), what's drawn sharp over it (`overlay.ts`) and its HUD (`hud.ts`, `game.css`). Its top bar is the Portal bar's shape (OFFICE · the project, **Return to Project**, the icons) and everything about the office itself is on its toolbar over the canvas (`pixel.html`, `pixel.css`). The windows both views open, the whiteboard and the bookshelf among them, are in `ui/`.
- **`pixel/`** is the 2D Office view's drawing and HUD (see above), and **`home/`**, **`firm/`**, **`mobile/`** and **`first-run/`** are the other pages' own parts.

### Stylesheets

Every page's sheet (`lite.css`, `pixel.css`, …) pulls in `styles/base.css` (the colors, the reset, panels, buttons and the window frame). Every other sheet sits next to its module and comes in with it. A module's sheet loads in no fixed order against `base.css`, so a module rule that overrides a base rule of the same specificity has to be more specific, or live at the end of `base.css` with the others there.

Colors are tokens: the custom properties at the top of `base.css`, each with what it's for (`--card` on `--paper`, `--line` round it and `--edge` under it, `--bad-ink` for red text, `--good-soft` behind good news, and so on). A sheet the 1D view loads names its colors with them rather than a hex, because the 1D view's color themes (`ui/colortheme.ts`, which sets `<html data-theme="dark">` or `"terminal"`) only change the tokens: their values are in `styles/themes.css`, and the Terminal theme's shapes (square corners, bracketed buttons, scanlines) in `styles/theme-terminal.css`. The Clean and Portal pairs are one family: Clean's rules (`styles/theme-clean*.css`, `ui/clean/` for the emoji) are under `html:is([data-theme^='clean'], [data-theme^='portal'])`, and Portal adds its tokens and parts (`styles/theme-portal*.css`), its top bar (`ui/portal/`, pieces marked `.pt-only` that only Portal shows), the 1D view's Portal layout (`ui/portal/layout.ts`: the left navigation `nav.ts` with its pure `nav-logic.ts`, the page header `pagehead.ts`, the Overview's cards `overview.ts` with `overview-logic.ts`; the page's own pieces it shows elsewhere are moved, not copied, by `slot.ts` and moved back in the other themes) and Home's Projects page (`home/portal.ts`, its filter and sort in `home/portal-logic.ts`). The values in `base.css` are the Default (Fun) look.

## Server

- **`server.ts`** is the composition root: `startServer` builds the office's context a stage at a time, starts the hook server, opens the floors, and hands the context to the HTTP handler and the WebSocket.
- **`office/`** is that context (`Ctx` in `office/context.ts`: the building-wide services, sending to browsers, the floors, the people in the office, going between floors, and the checks before something happens) and the pieces that make it.
- **`ws/`** takes the sockets. Each domain's messages are handled in `ws/handlers/<domain>.ts`, a map from message type to handler that `satisfies HandlerMap<ItsClientMsg>`, and `ws/handlers/index.ts` puts them all in one `HandlerMap<ClientMsg>`: a message type with no handler, or a handler for a type that doesn't exist, fails the typecheck. The same file lists the features that keep something per person on a floor (`features`: what each lets go of when someone is `leaving` a floor, and once their socket has `closed`) and the pieces of what someone arriving on a floor is sent (`views`, one for each field of `FloorView`, put together by `office/views.ts`).
- **`http/`** answers HTTP from a route table. Each file in `http/routes/` exports its routes, and `http/routes/index.ts` lists them in the order they're tried. Every route says its `auth` (`public`, or `session` for a signed-in browser), and the `Route` type won't let you leave it out.
- **`hooks/`** is the loopback-only hook server the workers call: their agents' hook events (`/hooks/<provider>`), the board agents' queue (`/office/queue`) and `office-workers` (`/office/workers`).
- **`workers/`** is the worker manager (`WorkerManager` in `workers/manager.ts`) and its pieces: worktrees, pull requests, tasks, terminals, ACP workers, and saving to `workers.json`. `src/server/workers.ts` re-exports it for the modules that imported it from there.
- **`providers/`** holds one adapter per agent CLI (see [Adding an agent provider](#adding-an-agent-provider)).
- **`tunnel/`** is `agent-office tunnel`, the one part that runs on someone's own computer instead of the office's: it asks the office for the workers' web servers (`/api/services`, in `http/routes/services.ts`) and listens on each one's port there. It imports nothing from the office but what the two say to each other (`tunnel/wire.ts`) and the cookie's name.

- **`flow/`** is the workflow engine, for work that's a list of steps that must survive failures and restarts (see [Workflows](site/automation/workflows.md)). A workflow (`WorkflowDef` in `flow/types.ts`) is named steps, each `async (ctx) => partial state update`, joined by edges: a step id, or a function of the state that picks one (a gate), with `END` to finish. `FlowEngine` (`flow/engine.ts`) runs it against one state per run and checkpoints the run (`RunRecord`: step, state, status, tries, loop counts, usage, history) through a `CheckpointStore` (`flow/store.ts`: `<data>/flows/<workflow>/<run>.json`, written atomically) after every step; a run that was `running` when the office stopped loads as `interrupted`. A step may declare `done(state)` (skipped when true, so resuming is safe), a `retry` policy (`flow/retry.ts`: exponential backoff with jitter, `retryOn`, e.g. `transientError`) and a `cache` (a key and a TTL). Loop guards (`limits.maxSteps`, `limits.loops['from->to']`) and budgets (`limits.budget`, `limits.check`, against what steps `report`) stop a run as `needs-attention`; a step returning `interrupt(payload)` stops it as `waiting` until `resume(runId, value)` hands the value to that step. Its events (`run-started`, `step-started`, `step-finished`, `step-failed`, `run-paused`, `run-finished`) are what the audit log (`auditFlows` in `flow/index.ts`) and anything else subscribe to. `flowsOf(ctx)` is the office's one engine; a feature registers its workflow there and starts runs, as the wizard's `JobBook` (`wizard/job.ts`) does, and `GET /api/flows` (`http/routes/flows.ts`) lists them. The engine is meant to grow without changing that shape: typed interrupts with options (`waiting.payload`, the `run-paused` event), shared state with reducers (`WorkflowDef.merge`), a pipeline whose gates are conditional edges, tracing (the events and `history`), and forking a run from a checkpoint (`HistoryEntry.seq`).

Planned Knowledge (the Library) and Evals work, and the existing audit, delivery and grader surfaces it builds on, are mapped with file paths in [Knowledge & Evals: Phase 0 gap map](knowledge-evals/gap-map.md).

The rest of `src/server/` is a module per service or per thing a floor keeps (`dog.ts`, `jukebox.ts`, `queue.ts`, `meetings.ts`), made by the office or by each `Floor` (`floor.ts`).

## Shared

- **`protocol.ts`** is the wire protocol. It puts the domain files in `protocol/<domain>.ts` back together (`export *`) and makes the `ClientMsg` and `ServerMsg` unions every frame is one of. Keep `import type` between domain files: some name each other's types (`floors.ts` and `presence.ts` do), which only type imports can do without a cycle at run time.
- **`providers.ts`** is the provider table: every agent a worker can run, and what the office knows about each.

The rest is data and pure code both sides use: the floor's layout, maps (`shared/maps/`), the sun, the garage and so on.

## Adding a feature

A new feature adds files of its own and one line in each list it joins. For something on a floor that everyone there sees, that's:

1. **Its messages** in a protocol domain file (`src/shared/protocol/<domain>.ts`). A new domain file also goes in `protocol.ts`: its `import type`, its `export *` and its members of the unions.
2. **A server handler file**, `src/server/ws/handlers/<name>.ts`, and its line in `handlers` in `ws/handlers/index.ts`. If it keeps something per person, its `FeatureHooks` go in `features` there; if people arriving on a floor need its state, that's a field of `FloorView` (`protocol/floors.ts`) and its piece in `views`. What a floor keeps is a module of its own in `src/server/`, which `Floor` makes.
3. **A state slice**, `src/client/state/slices/<name>.ts`, and its line at the end of `SLICES` in `state/slices/index.ts`.
4. **Its window or panel** in `src/client/ui/<name>/` (or `ui/<name>.ts`), opened from the page that shows it: a tab of the 1D view (`lite.ts`), an item of the ☰ menu (`shared/flatmenu.ts`), or something to click in the 2D Office view (`pixel/hotspots.ts`).
5. **Its CSS** next to its ui module, imported from there, in the color tokens.

Its HTTP routes, if it has any, go in `http/routes/`, and its tests in `tests/`.

## Adding an agent provider

One adapter file in `src/server/providers/`, one entry in `PROVIDERS` in `src/server/providers/index.ts`, and one row in `src/shared/providers.ts` (its id in `AGENT_PROVIDERS`, its entry in `PROVIDER_META`). The typecheck fails until all three are there. Its row says which models and efforts it takes and how the hire dialog asks for them; one whose CLI lists its models gets a lister in `src/server/models.ts` too. What reads them, and the one place that still names providers one by one, are in [Provider seams](dsh-acp-integration.md#provider-seams).

## The size guard

`tests/size.test.ts` holds every `.ts` and `.css` file under `src/` to 600 lines. It asks `git ls-files` for them (tracked, and new ones that aren't ignored) rather than walking the folder, so a worktree checked out inside the repo can't trip it. The files that were longer when it came in are listed in `CEILINGS`, each with the length it had then: they may shrink, but never grow past it. Once one is down to 600 lines or fewer, or gone, the test fails until you take it off the list, so the list only gets shorter.

When it fails, split the file along the registries: a feature's code goes in its folder, a message handler in its domain's file, a tick or a key in the feature it's for, and what the page keeps of it in its own slice. Raising a ceiling, or adding a file to the list, only hands the problem to the next person, so the honest way to make room is to split the file. When you shrink a listed file, lower its ceiling to its new length in the same change, so it can't grow back.
